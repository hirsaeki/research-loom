# Quantitative and Figure/Table Writing

Use with approved quantitative results and approved figure/table metadata.

## Required order of work

1. State briefly what the reader should inspect when a lead-in is useful.
2. Make the reading frame visible: n, denominator, multiple-response status, unit, period, or other supplied metadata as applicable.
3. Describe only the principal approved values, gaps, ranks, or changes needed for the argument.
4. Put observation before approved interpretation.
5. Put approved sample scope, applicability, uncertainty, and measurement limits near the relevant result.
6. Connect to the next section only through a supplied approved connection.

## Strength control

- Descriptive observation stays descriptive.
- A supplied interpretation may be rendered with strength calibrated to its approved status (e.g. shows / suggests / may indicate).
- Do not convert a survey association into a causal claim.
- Do not infer population-wide validity from a limited sample.
- If the user asks for causal/general validity and no approved judgment exists, return `[NEEDS_ACADEMIC_QA: ...]`.

## Figure/table behavior

- Figure title below; table title above; use formal numbering and number-based references.
- Put source information close to external figures/tables/citations according to formal metadata.
- After a figure/table, do not narrate every cell.
- A model figure must also be explained in body text using only approved components/relations.
- If a color chart is used, run color-use and grayscale-distinguishability QA only when the applicable formal profile supplies those conditions.

## Primary Exposition Location (Home Chapter legacy alias)

If a survey/result has a declared `primary_exposition_map`, render its primary result there. Accept `home_chapter_map` as the legacy alias. In downstream model/recommendation sections, reference the already-approved result/finding; do not re-open the survey data or discover a new reading.
